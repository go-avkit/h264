go-avkit/h264 (main with the Inverse8x8 and motionSegments changes) against ffmpeg 6.1.1, decode alone,
AMD Ryzen 9 7950X, one core (taskset -c 9), 7 interleaved rounds per clip. The 1-minute, 5-minute and
15-minute load averages (/proc/loadavg) are read before EACH round (the host was otherwise idle: a single
pinned decode adds about 1.0 to the 1-minute figure only after a minute, hence the small values).

What each side times (the ratio is not like for like, and slightly favours go-avkit/h264):
  go-avkit/h264: wall time of the decode inside the process (tools/clip --nohash): after the file is read
                 and the process is running, no demux, no output.
  ffmpeg:        utime + stime of the whole process (-benchmark), so it includes process start, the demuxer
                 (raw Annex B probing) and the null muxer. That fixed cost is measured at the end: about
                 0.011 s for a tiny stream, i.e. 1.2 % of ffmpeg's 0.94 s on alien and 3.6 % of its 0.30 s on
                 fade. Subtracting it moves the ratios (ffmpeg time / ours) from 0.31x to 0.30x on alien
                 (0.94/3.03 to 0.93/3.03) and from 0.265x to 0.254x on fade (0.301/1.136 to 0.290/1.136);
                 they are not subtracted in the table below.
